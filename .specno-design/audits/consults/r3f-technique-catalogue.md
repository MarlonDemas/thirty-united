## Technique catalogue: anime matchday poster RSVP

### Baseline found in the repo (this changes several answers)

- **Installed 3D stack:** only `three@0.186.1` (and `@types/three`), `@react-three/fiber@9.8.1`, `motion@14.1.1` and React 19.2. Nothing else is present.
  - **Not installed:** `@react-three/drei`, `@react-three/postprocessing`, `postprocessing`, `gsap`, `theatre`.
  - **`src/components/three/`** holds only the lanyard ticket: `LanyardTicket.tsx` (344 lines), `rope.ts`, `ticketTexture.ts`, `sceneTextures.ts`, `DebugOverlay.tsx`.
  - **Existing patterns you can reuse:**
    - Canvas-2D `CanvasTexture`s with a `cssVar()` token reader.
    - `invalidate()`-style on-demand rendering.
    - A `?debug=1` FPS overlay.
    - A lazy-loaded chunk after first paint, gated by `hasWebGL()` and reduced-motion.
- **CSP in `netlify.toml`:** `default-src 'self'; script-src 'self'; img-src 'self' data:; font-src 'self'; connect-src 'self' https://*.convex.cloud wss://*.convex.cloud`. There is no `worker-src`, `wasm-unsafe-eval` or `blob:`. Effects on this work:
  - **Blocked: blob Web Workers.** `worker-src` falls back to `script-src 'self'`. This kills troika-three-text and any `?worker` import that Vite inlines as a blob.
  - **Blocked: WASM.** This kills Draco and Basis/KTX2 transcoders, and drei's default `useGLTF` Draco loader fetches from gstatic anyway.
  - **Blocked: `blob:` images.** `img-src` has `data:` but not `blob:`. Anything that does `URL.createObjectURL` into an `<img>` fails. Use `canvas.toDataURL()` for the poster preview, or add `blob:` to `img-src`.
  - **Blocked: external assets.** Fonts and textures must be self-hosted.
  - **Still fine:** shaders, `toBlob`, anchor downloads, and `navigator.share({files})` (no CSP involvement).
- **Recommendation:** the whole plan below needs zero CSP change if you follow the "no worker/WASM/blob-image" rule. The one possible edit is adding `blob:` to `img-src`, and I would avoid it.
- **Sizes below** are my estimates of gzipped bundle contribution, not measured. I did not run installs or a bundle analysis. Verify with `vite build` plus a visualizer before committing.

### Core design stance

The most mobile-friendly way to get this look is **not** a real 3D stadium. It is a **stack of painted 2D layers rendered by one orthographic or perspective R3F scene with a handful of custom fullscreen/plane shaders**.

- **Assets:** sky, moon, stadium silhouette, floodlights, hero cutout, foreground crowd and smoke plates, pre-baked from AI art with depth maps.
- **Procedural parts:** light cones, embers, haze and clouds.
- **Rendering model:** few draw calls and no real lights, shadows or geometry.
- **Why this suits the target:** it keeps the scene cheap in the WhatsApp WebView (iOS WKWebView / Android System WebView). It is also the only approach that gives an actual illustrated anime look, since toon-shading a 3D stadium never looks like a poster.

Cheap and looks right. Option B: screen-space radial blur god rays from the light's projected position (use `three-stdlib`-style radial-blur, or a custom pass) over an occlusion mask. Option C: `postprocessing`'s `GodRaysEffect`. It needs an occluder source mesh and a full extra pass, so it is heavy on mobile. Pair cones with the bloom from #10 for the glow. | **Code-only** (cones and radial blur). | 4 | **Smoke / haze: sprites, noise shader, fog** | Three options, combinable. (a) **Sprite/plane smoke:** 10–30 large soft-alpha planes with a baked smoke texture, slow rotation, UV drift and fade, additive or normal blend, tinted red from the pitch flares. Cheapest, and works as parallax layers. (b) **Noise-shader haze:** a fullscreen or per-layer plane with fbm noise (3–4 octaves of value or simplex noise in GLSL) scrolled over time, multiplied by a height gradient, to get a ground haze or fog bank. Medium cost, fully procedural. (c) **Scene `Fog`/`FogExp2`:** works only on real 3D meshes with built-in materials and does nothing for layered sprites; use only for depth tinting of any real geometry. (b) and (c) are **code-only**. | (a) low-medium (overdraw). (b) medium, scales with pixel count; run at half resolution on a smaller render target if needed. (c) negligible. | Sprites: 0.5 day. fbm haze: 0.5–1 day. |
| 5 | **GPU ember particles** | `THREE.Points` with `BufferGeometry` holding per-particle seed attributes (`aSeed`, `aSpeed`, `aSize`) and a vertex shader computing position from `uTime`: `y = fract(seed + uTime * speed)` rising, plus sine sway, plus size and alpha curves. No CPU update loop, no `useFrame` per-particle cost, so one draw call. 600–1500 points is plenty and safe on mobile; set `gl_PointSize` scaled by dpr and clamp to the GPU's max (often 64 or less on mobile). Use `drawRange` or a count driven by quality tier (#12). Optional second layer of large blurred foreground embers for depth-of-field feel. | **Code-only.** | Low: one draw call, no texture needed. Fill rate rises with point size, so keep embers small and few. | 3–5 hours. |
| 6 | **Moonlit sky with drifting clouds** | Sky: a fullscreen plane or large background quad with a vertical gradient shader (deep navy to blue-white at horizon) plus a hash-based star field (dither the stars so they do not shimmer). Moon: a textured disc with a soft radial glow shader behind it, plus a halo sprite. Clouds: either (a) 3–5 layered planes with a painted cloud-bank texture, each scrolling at its own speed (parallax, cheapest, best anime look), or (b) an fbm cloud shader lit from the moon's direction with a rim-light term. Do not use drei's volumetric `Clouds` (instanced sprites, heavy overdraw, and needs drei). | Moon disc texture and cloud-bank plates **need art** if you want the anime-painted look (AI generation is fine); the gradient, stars, glow and fbm clouds are **code-only**. | Low for gradient/stars/glow. With only 4–5 discrete chapters, define a typed array of camera "shots" (`position`, `lookAt`/`target`, `fov`, plus per-layer uniforms like parallax strength) and animate the one shared camera in `useFrame` using `motion`'s `animate()` (already installed) or a damped lerp. Options compared: **drei `CameraControls`** (~camera-controls, ~10 KB gz) is built for user orbit, which is not wanted here. **GSAP** (~25 KB gz core) is fine but duplicates `motion`. **theatre.js** (`@theatre/core` + `@theatre/r3f`, roughly 100 KB+ gz, with a studio only in dev) is only worth it if the owner wants to author the camera visually in a timeline editor. Recommendation: `motion` plus a small `shots.ts`. | **Code-only.** | Negligible. | 0.5–1 day for the shot system and tuning. Add 1–2 days only if theatre.js is chosen for visual authoring. |
| 8 | **Manga-panel or page-turn chapter transitions** | Two good approaches. (a) **Panel wipe:** a fullscreen transition plane with a shader that reveals the next chapter through a slanted panel mask (`step()` on a rotated coordinate, with a thick black border and slight white gutter), animated by a `uProgress` uniform; render the outgoing and incoming chapter into two render targets and mix, or simply draw the incoming chapter over the outgoing through the mask (cheaper, no RTs). (b) **DOM overlay panels:** cut the screen into 3–5 CSS `clip-path: polygon()` panels animated with `motion`, with the canvas continuing underneath. This is the cheapest, easiest to keep accessible, and honours `prefers-reduced-motion` trivially. (c) Page-turn curl: a subdivided plane with a vertex-shader cylinder bend; looks great but needs both pages as textures (two render targets), so costlier. My recommendation is (b) for most chapters, with (a) for one hero transition. | **Code-only.** An ink-splash or speed-line texture sheet would be nice, but speed lines are easy to draw procedurally. | (b) negligible GPU. (a) low (one fullscreen shader, no RTs) or medium with two RTs. (c) medium. | (b): 0.5–1 day for 4–5 variants. (a): 0.5–1 day. (c): 1–1.5 days. |
| 9 | **Animated type: MSDF text in 3D, brush-stroke reveal** | **Avoid troika-three-text** (and therefore drei `<Text>`): it builds SDF atlases in a blob Web Worker and fetches a font from a CDN, so the current CSP blocks both. Options that fit: (a) **Canvas-2D to `CanvasTexture`** for MATCHDAY, name and number: self-hosted fonts already available (`@fontsource/barlow-condensed`), render at high resolution once, put on a plane. No worker, no WASM, matches the existing `ticketTexture.ts` approach. Use a heavy condensed weight and draw an offset red shadow plus stroke for the poster look. (b) **Real MSDF** via `three-msdf-text-utils` (small, ~5 KB gz) with an msdf-bmfont-xml atlas you generate once at build time and ship as static files (no worker, no WASM at runtime); crisper at any scale and cheap to animate per glyph. Worth it only if type must stay razor sharp while the camera pushes in. (c) **Brush-stroke reveal:** use the text or brush-script texture as an alpha mask and sweep a threshold across a brush-stroke alpha texture: `alpha = step(strokeNoise, uProgress)` where `strokeNoise` is a greyscale map whose value is the time each pixel gets painted (generate procedurally in Canvas-2D by drawing a thick jittered path with a gradient along its length, so it reveals left to right with ragged edges). For the brush-script name use a self-hosted brush font (needs a licensed or OFL font file; do not hotlink, and note fonts from Google need self-hosting for CSP). | Brush-script font file: **needs asset** (pick an OFL font, self-host). | 10 | **Bloom, grain and colour grading for the anime look** | **Without adding a library:** write one combined custom fullscreen pass in plain three (`EffectComposer` from `three/examples/jsm` is in the installed `three`, with `UnrealBloomPass`, `OutputPass` and a `ShaderPass`). The look comes from a small set of cheap operations: threshold-and-blur bloom at half or quarter resolution (a cheap dual-Kawase or simple separable blur), film grain (hash noise with `uTime`), a vignette, a lift/gamma/gain split-tone (shadows pushed to blue-black, highlights to warm red), and slight chromatic aberration. `UnrealBloomPass` is multi-mip and heavy; a single-pass half-res bloom is lighter. **With a library:** `@react-three/postprocessing` plus `postprocessing` (about 60–70 KB gz for the `postprocessing` core plus R3F wrapper) is the easiest, merges effects into one pass automatically (`Bloom`, `Noise`, `Vignette`, `ToneMapping`, `LUT`), and is worth it if you also use #2 and #3 as effects. Grain, vignette and grade are cheap ALU. | Combined custom pass: 1 day. Using `postprocessing`: 0.5 day plus install and bundle impact. |

**Libraries that would force a CSP change:** troika-three-text (blob worker; needs `worker-src blob:`), anything using Draco or Basis/KTX2 (needs `'wasm-unsafe-eval'`), transformers.js or onnxruntime-web (WASM plus worker plus external model fetch). All avoidable. The recommended plan adds no WASM and no workers.

### Overall budget and plan

- **Realistic render stack on a mid-range phone:** 5–7 alpha layers, 4–6 additive cone quads, one embers `Points` draw, 10–20 smoke planes, one hand-rolled half-res bloom plus grade pass. This should hold 30 fps; 60 fps on newer phones. Cap dpr at 1.5 by default.
- **Biggest GPU risks, in order:** bloom, full-screen fbm haze/clouds, additive overdraw of big smoke or cone quads, then depth-parallax fragment shaders at high dpr.
- **Biggest non-GPU risks:**
  1. **Asset production.** A coherent set of layered, depth-mapped art is the critical path and decides the look more than any shader. Plan 2–4 days of AI generation, cut-out and depth work.
  2. **WhatsApp WebView behaviour:** gyro permission prompts on iOS, no downloads, and context loss when the view is backgrounded. Test on real devices early.
  3. **Texture memory.** Many 2K layers add up (a 2048² RGBA texture is about 22 MB on the GPU with mipmaps). Use 1024–1536 px layers, WebP, no mipmaps for fullscreen planes, and reuse one texture atlas where possible. Note that KTX2 compression, which would help, needs WASM, so skip it.
- **Rough total effort for the whole catalogue** (code only, assets provided): about 7–10 working days for a single experienced R3F developer, plus asset production and device tuning. Suggested build order for early risk reduction: (1) layered parallax rig with real art plus the quality-tier system, (2) type and poster export with the WhatsApp save/share path, (3) embers, cones, haze, (4) camera shots and transitions, (5) post stack and grade.
