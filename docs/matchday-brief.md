# matchday-rsvp (approved brief)

Build an RSVP experience that plays like a match day, called "Matchday RSVP", as a Vite + Three.js r186 scene in worlds/matchday-rsvp/.

EVENT (✎ all editable, in worlds/matchday-rsvp/config.js, the one file an organiser edits; flow.js re-exports `EVENT` and `FIELDS` but is not where you edit them): title "Save the Date: Matchday", date "Saturday 12 December 2026", kick-off "18:00", venue "Kick-Off Arena", host "The Organising Committee", short date "12.12" (the LED board's "RSVP • 12.12"). Home kit #C8102E red with white ✎; away kit #1B2A4A navy ✎ (`EVENT.kits`). FIELDS (✎ one `FIELDS` array, one goal each): Full name, Email, Mobile number, Guests joining you (0-3). Also in config.js: `EVENT.privacy` (the POPIA notice values), `NOTICE_VERSION` and `THEME` (colours, type, motion, scene tuning).

BEFORE GO-LIVE:
- [ ] Fill the four `EVENT.privacy` values in `src/matchday/config.js` (`responsibleParty`, `contact`, `retention`, `policyUrl`). Production stays closed while any placeholder remains.
- [ ] Set `RETENTION_DAYS` in `convex/admin.ts` to the retention period stated in `EVENT.privacy.retention`.
- [ ] Set `DECLINES_OPEN` to `"true"` on production (`incredible-crane-757`) and on the preview deployment (`modest-marten-55`), and redeploy the preview backend so it has the declines code. Until it is set, the name-only decline answers `service_unavailable`.
- [ ] Keep `NOTICE_VERSION` in `src/matchday/config.js` inside the server's `DECLINE_NOTICE_VERSIONS` allow-list in `convex/lib/declineValidation.ts`: any bump changes both. Filling the privacy placeholders before launch needs no bump, because nobody has seen v2 in production yet.
- [ ] Bump `NOTICE_VERSION` whenever the notice wording changes after go-live (format `/^[a-z0-9.-]{1,32}$/`).
- [ ] Confirm the Netlify site's environment variables do not set `NODE_ENV` (the build refuses a non-production `NODE_ENV`).
- [ ] Open question for the owner: guests who replied before the privacy notice existed. Decide whether to send them the notice.
The dev server skips the privacy check, so a dev run does not show that closed state.

WORLD: an evening football stadium at real-world scale, about 30,000 seats. Landmarks:
(1) entrance plaza with a brick-and-steel facade, a ticket-reader turnstile bank and a floodlit "KICK-OFF ARENA" sign;
(2) a players' tunnel from the concourse onto the pitch;
(3) the pitch, 105 × 68 m plus a 6 m run-off, with mown stripes, white markings, penalty spots and arcs;
(4) two goals (7.32 × 2.44 m) with net geometry that ripples on a goal;
(5) home and away dugouts with perspex roofs;
(6) a two-tier rounded-rectangle seating bowl (lower 25 rows, upper 20 rows, 0.8 m row depth, about 0.4 m rise per row) with instanced seats in the home colour, aisles, vomitories and railings;
(7) a cantilever roof with visible steel trusses and an open centre;
(8) four corner floodlight masts (about 50 m) plus a roof-edge LED strip;
(9) a giant scoreboard screen on the north stand (canvas texture, emissive);
(10) LED advertising boards around the pitch in the accent colour;
(11) corner flags;
(12) the crowd: instanced low-poly spectators with seeded fill (84% of lower-tier seats, 70% of upper-tier seats), mostly home-colour shirts (78%, plus white, away navy and dark), scarves.
A goalkeeper and a penalty taker: try a CC0/CC-BY rigged footballer on Sketchfab via the Blender MCP (licence check, `npm run optimize`, CREDITS line). If Blender isn't connected, say so in one line and build them in code (PBR kit materials, never a capsule-only mannequin). Hero assets from assets/: street_lamp_01 on the entrance plaza. Everything else is built in code with PBR materials. The ball is a code-built truncated-icosahedron pattern.
Realism moves required, because the stadium is a building and primitive-only buildings are banned: concrete_floor_02 on the stand risers and concourse, corrugated_iron on the roof underside and facade panels, asphalt_02 on the plaza, leafy_grass on the pitch with a procedural stripe multiply, a normal map on the facade, visible structural members.
Budget: ≤ 50k tris per instanced prop. Crowd ≤ 500k tris total in 1–2 draw calls, `castShadow = false`. Seats in 1 instanced draw call. Whole scene ≤ about 1.5M tris.

LOOK: preset realistic. HDRI assets/hdri/qwantani_dusk_2_puresky_2k.hdr (fetch with `npm run asset hdri qwantani_dusk_2_puresky 2k`), via HDRLoader + EquirectangularReflectionMapping + PMREM, as scene.environment and background (background intensity 0.5). It is pure sky, so it doesn't clash with the code-built bowl, unlike stadium_01. The sky the guest sees is a warm dusk gradient dome (zenith #1a2140, mid #4a3a55, horizon #d98a5f); the HDRI lights the scene. Fog and the horizon band take the dome's horizon colour mixed toward the ink, not the measured horizonHex. One shadow-casting sun, with its vector measured at build time from `npm run sun` (the SUN constant in scene.js). The sun is low, so the stands shadow the pitch and the floodlights carry it: non-shadow SpotLights from each mast, with emissive lamp heads in the SelectiveBloomEffect selection (bloom on emissives only: lamps, LED boards, scoreboard). PCFShadowMap + shadow.radius, N8AOPostPass after RenderPass, ToneMappingEffect AGX last (set by `THEME.scene.toneMapping`), sRGB output, DPR cap 2. A lite tier (`?lite`, 4 or fewer CPU cores, or Save-Data) drops the ambient occlusion, uses DPR 1 and a 2048 shadow map. Accent colour #C8102E ✎ (3D accent; the overlay uses the lighter kit red #ff5a6e). Mood: a warm dusk sky over a floodlit, packed bowl. No reference images supplied.

LIFE: the crowd idles with per-instance seeded bob and arm sway in the vertex shader. On a goal: a 3 s jump-and-cheer burst, scarves up, and a scoreboard flash. If the last HOME station is a goal, the crowd roars "Siuuu" as the guest lands. At full time: a Mexican wave around the bowl, then confetti and fireworks over the roof. The keeper sways on his line before each kick. The corner flags flutter and the players have an idle sway. HOME teammates warm up on the pitch: two passing pairs jog fixed loops, and a few more stretch near the stations. On AWAY the floodlights dim and the crowd applauds.

MOOD:
- HOME is triumphant. Your name goes up on the scoreboard, a last goal sends you sprinting to the corner flag for the Siuuu as the crowd roars it back, teammates mob you there, the floodlights blaze, and full time brings the Mexican wave and fireworks.
- AWAY is bittersweet and warm. The floodlights dim to about 60%, the crowd and the HOME players applaud you off at the touchline, and the scoreboard reads "We'll miss you, <name>".

INTERACTION: mode open-roam. Third person: the guest plays as their own footballer, with the camera on a boom behind and over the right shoulder (4.2 m back, 0.35 m right).
1. **Invitation and side.** The scene loads on the entrance plaza. The 3D invitation ticket (canvas texture with the EVENT details and the home crest) floats in front of the camera. The turnstile bank is split into two lanes with floodlit signs: **HOME END** (home red) and **AWAY END** (away navy), each with its own kiosk. Walking into a lane and using its kiosk is choosing your side: HOME means "Yes, I'll save the date", AWAY means "I'll be away on the date". Football grounds split home and away entrances the same way.
2. **Movement.** On desktop, WASD or the arrow keys walk, Shift runs, and dragging orbits the camera round the guest (360° yaw, pitch clamped to 80°). The footballer faces where it moves. On phones, tapping the ground walks there; a tap is under 8 px of movement and under 250 ms. Tapping a station walks to it, and dragging orbits the camera. Movement keys never act while the guest is typing in a card.
3. **Walkable area.** The plaza, the chosen lane, the tunnel corridor, and the pitch plus run-off. The stands are blocked, and the goals, dugouts and corner flags are obstacles.
4. **Guide.** A lit beacon stands at the next unfinished station, and the scoreboard names it ("Next: Email · HOME dugout"). A centred "Walk to next station" button walks the guest there; it also serves keyboard and screen-reader guests.
5. **Station cards.** Each station's question also shows on an in-world board. Arriving within 3 m opens a **centred** card with the input (validated: email format, mobile digits; guests is a four-button radiogroup 0/1/2/3 with arrow-key selection), "Take the challenge" and "Skip the kick". During a challenge the card hides, and a slim top-centre chip shows the instruction plus a visible Skip.
6. **Never blocking.** Every challenge is optional. A skip saves the answer straight away. There are at most 2 attempts, and after one failure the next always succeeds. HOME stations 2-4 can be done in any order.

Stations:

| # | Where | Field | Challenge |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Plaza, the HOME or AWAY lane kiosk (both sides) | Name + POPIA notice (side wording) | Hand your ticket into that lane's reader (drag or click the ticket). The arm turns, the light goes green, the gate opens, then a cinematic walk-out through the tunnel. |
| 2 | HOME dugout (x −37.4, z +8) | Email | **Dribble and shoot:** a teammate lays the ball off, then a 5-cone slalom from the dugout toward the **south** goal (z +52.5), with a second keeper. The run is an automatic jog: Left/Right (A/D) or a sideways drag steer, Up/Down (W/S) speed up or ease off, and Space or a tap shoots once the last cone is passed. Past the cones the run carries on toward the goal with the ball at your feet, so you shoot on the move; left alone it eases to a stop 12 m from the goal (clear of the keeper) and shooting stays open. Clipping a cone knocks it over and slows you, never a fail. The shot goes across the keeper to the far post (an arrow held picks the corner); a shot at the near post is saved ("Saved. Take it again"), and after one failure the next scores. The retake starts where you shot, still running if you were. The teammate by the tunnel runs over to celebrate. |
| 3 | North-east corner flag under the scoreboard | Mobile | **Corner and a teammate header:** a ring on the grass marks the striker's zone; aim the cross by drag (release crosses) or the arrow keys (Space crosses). A cross into the ring is headed in by the HOME striker past the north keeper. A cross short of or beyond the ring is the one failure ("Too short. Take it again" / "Too long. Take it again"), and the retake is pulled into the ring. The camera follows the cross to the header. |
| 4 | North penalty spot (0, −41.5) | Guests (0-3) | **Penalty:** drag to aim and release to shoot (a release without a drag shows "Drag to aim, then release"); keyboard: the four arrow keys aim, Space shoots. The keeper dives to a seeded side. Shots to either top corner always score, the keeper only saves shots within reach of his dive side, and after one failed shot the next one scores. A save or a miss gives a friendly toast ("Saved. Take it again", "Over the bar. Take it again" or "Wide. Take it again"). |

A goal ripples the net, makes the crowd cheer and roar, and updates the scoreboard (HOME n–0 AWAY with a flipping digit). Every finished station, played or skipped, adds a point.

7. **Full time (HOME).** If the last station ends in a goal, the guest sprints to the nearest corner flag and does the Siuuu (a few quick steps, a jump with a half turn, landing facing the pitch, legs wide, arms down and back, chest out) and the huddle forms there. A skipped last station leads into the huddle where the guest stands. Then the whistle, the scoreboard "FULL TIME · HOME 4–0 · YOU WIN", the Mexican wave, fireworks and confetti, then the reply is sent (see Data). On success the final card reads "Your RSVP is confirmed" (kicker "Full time · HOME 4–0"), "Await your invitation with further details." The card shows the guest's details back to them.
8. **AWAY ending.** One field (full name) at the AWAY END kiosk, then the gate and the tunnel, then the AWAY dugout touchline (x −37.4, z −8). The floodlights dim to about 60%, the crowd applauds, the scoreboard reads "AWAY FAN · We'll miss you, <name>", then the reply is sent. On success the final card reads "Thanks for letting us know." and "We'll miss you, <name>."
9. **Cinematic.** Every move eases in and out on the sim clock.
   - **Tunnel walk-out (hero shot):** the guest pushes through the open turnstile and dips to black. A slow push follows up the darkened players' tunnel, where the floodlight glow (bloom and light level) builds and the muffled crowd opens up toward the mouth. On exit a crane rises over the south goal to reveal the packed bowl and the scoreboard, then settles on the boom behind the guest. HOME roars on exit; AWAY cranes along the touchline to its dugout.
   - **Station arrivals:** a 2.2 s curved walk-in around the station, which ends on the boom behind the guest, looking at the sign and teammate, with a slight FOV push-in (58° to 55°).
   - **Goal moments:** the camera tracks the ball. On the scoring dribble shot, the header and the penalty goal, play slows to 0.35× for about 0.8 s around impact, and a low-pass filter closes on the audio. On the dribble shot the held camera also swings 1.5 m wider over the right shoulder (and 0.4 m up) through the strike and flight, easing back once the ball is in, so the footballer's own body never hides the shot (reduced motion cuts to the wide framing).
   - **Full time (HOME):** after a last goal, the camera tracks the sprint to the corner from behind and to the side, then sits low in front of the flag for the jump and the landing. A crane then lifts off the corner huddle (or off the huddle where the guest stands) and sweeps up toward "FULL TIME · HOME 4–0 · YOU WIN". Reduced motion cuts straight to the pose.
   - **AWAY:** a slow pull-back from the dugout touchline toward the scoreboard as the floodlights dim.
   - **Polish:** a gentle, seeded handheld sway while walking. Thin letterbox bars (7% of the height) slide in only during cinematic moves and slow-motion beats, never while a card or challenge chip is up.
   - **Timing:** an unskipped HOME run has about 30 s of skippable forced animation (measured 29.7 s; budget 30 s), plus about 7.5 s of ball flights and post-goal waits that are the guest's own result.
   - **Skip and reduced motion:** skip always wins. Tap, click, Space, Enter or Escape jumps every move, slow-motion beat and the bars to their end. Reduced motion gets cuts instead of moves, with no sway, slow motion or bars.
10. **Fast lane ("Skip the game, just reply", a secondary control in the top-left corner).** One card with one form: "Can you make it?" (Attending / Can't make it, a radiogroup), then the fields for that side, one Send. It goes through the same send path as the game. HOME shows the full-time scoreboard moment before sending; AWAY shows the AWAY scoreboard.
11. **No-WebGL page.** If WebGL2 is missing, the scene fails to load, or a lost GL context is not restored within 3 s, the scene is replaced by a plain HTML page: the event title, When / Where / Host, and the same fast-lane form. It sends through the same path and shows the same confirmation card. An RSVP the game already sent shows its confirmation there, not a fresh form.
Privacy: a POPIA short notice ("How we use your details", from `EVENT.privacy`) shows before any personal field: on the name kiosk card in either lane, the fast lane and the no-WebGL page. In the game's cards it is a collapsed `<details>` on phones (560 px wide or less) and open on wider screens. The wording follows the side (AWAY says name only). "Full notice" links to `policyUrl` once it starts with `https://`.
Closed state: in production, when `EVENT.privacy` still has placeholders or `policyUrl` is not https, every entry point shows "RSVPs open soon" with Send disabled, and sends nothing.
Data: held in memory only (no storage). One send path (submit.js) for the game, the fast lane and the no-WebGL page. State runs idle → sending → sent | failed, and the fast lane locks its answers after the first send. The send is a Convex call (see the addendum). The payload carries `notice` (`NOTICE_VERSION`). The confirmation card renders only on `sent`; while a network send is pending the card reads "Sending your reply…", and on failure "Reply not sent" with a Send button. `window.__rsvp` and the other debug hooks (`window.__world`, `window.__dbg.info`, `window.__dbg.flow`) exist on the dev server only; a production build has none of them. All user text is rendered via textContent or canvas fillText, never innerHTML. Mute toggle top-right; procedural WebAudio crowd roar (no audio assets).

DASHBOARD: none.

EXTRAS (2): procedural WebAudio crowd ambience with goal roars and a whistle; full-time fireworks + confetti (instanced, seeded).

CONTRACT: `window.__ready` after assets load + one frame. `window.__views` has 5 entries, each `{name, pos, target, fov, stage}` (1-3 also `feet`, the footballer's spot; `pos` is the boom camera), and `__setView(i)` also applies the stage, so the capture sheet shows the real experience: 0 `gates` (the plaza, both lanes and both END signs under the floating invitation ticket; the guest's footballer stands off to the HOME side, clear of the signs and the ticket, feet above the card, on wide and phone screens), 1 `dribble` (mid-slalom, behind the guest's footballer: ball at its feet, cones, the south keeper in goal), 2 `corner` (behind the guest's footballer at the flag, run-up beside the ball: ball, the striker's ring, the striker in the box, the north goal), 3 `penalty` (behind the guest's footballer, 1.5 m back from the spot, challenge chip up, keeper and goal and crowd behind), 4 `aerial` (the whole bowl, beacon visible). In a production build `__setView` only stages `gates` and `aerial`, and only before the guest has walked off the intro; once the guest has entered anything it returns `null` and changes nothing, and a staged view never sends. `window.__dbg.simStep(dt)` advances the crowd, keeper, ball, teammates and the guest's walk deterministically. `agents()` returns the teammates who move: 2 passing pairs on fixed, separated warm-up loops, with `related` set to each partner, plus, while active, the dribble's second keeper (`keeperSouth`) and ball (`dribbleBall`), the corner's striker (`striker`) and ball (`cornerBall`), each related to its ball or keeper, the celebrating teammate (`tunnel`) while he runs, and the four huddle teammates (`huddle1`-`huddle4`, related to each other) while they jog in, at the corner after the Siuuu. `npm run smoke` must report 0 overlaps and 0 stuck. In the no-WebGL page `__views` is `[]` and `__setView` and `simStep` are no-ops; `window.__fallback` is true there. Seeded mulberry32 RNG, no Math.random. The primary card is centred or in-world, and in every aim or challenge stage the ball and the goal mouth stay on screen and clear of any overlay.

VERIFY: `npm run capture matchday-rsvp` + `npm run smoke matchday-rsvp`. Read sheet.png. Score: scale reads right, lighting matches realistic dusk-under-floodlights, no overlaps or z-fighting (pitch lines, seats), smoke 0 overlaps / 0 stuck, no console errors, ≥ 50 fps at 1080p. ## Addendum (2026-11-03)

- The event is Makes's 30th: Monday 21 December 2026, venue to be announced, no kick-off time. The host speaks in the first person and signs "Makes". No club, crest or stadium names.
- The RSVP backend is Convex, called with ConvexHttpClient (`otp.requestCode`, `rsvp.verifyAndSave`). The guest gets a 6-digit code by email at the end of the journey and the answers are saved only after it is confirmed. The old `rsvp:submit` event, `RSVP_ENDPOINT`, `RSVP_MODE` and the idempotency key were removed. The invite key is read once from `?k=`; a missing or rejected key shows a closed intro.
- Fields: attending (HOME yes, AWAY no, "Not sure yet" maybe), first name, surname, optional shirt name, email (dugout), sport and team (dugout, station 2), invite channel and WhatsApp (corner flag), plus-one and plus-one name (penalty spot). Fun fact and how-you-know-Makes are not asked. AWAY asks first name, surname and email, then the code.
- Story and look follow DESIGN.md: "You've been called up", tagline "Thirty feels like half time. Come for the oranges.", HOME kit shirt-red #9F0B19, AWAY kit dark neutral with moonlight trim, shield crest and arena signage replaced with a "30" seal and THIRTY / 21 DEC 2026 / MAKES · 30.
- "Just reply" is a first-class choice on the intro beside walking in.
